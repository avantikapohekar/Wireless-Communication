# 6. Explain FDMA, TDMA and CDMA. Compare these multiple access techniques.

In wireless communication, **Multiple Access Techniques** are protocols designed to allow multiple users or mobile stations to share a single, limited communication channel or radio spectrum simultaneously without causing mutual interference. The three primary techniques are **FDMA, TDMA, and CDMA**.

---

### **1. FDMA (Frequency Division Multiple Access)**
FDMA divides the available channel bandwidth into separate, non-overlapping **frequency bands**. 
*   **The Concept:** Each mobile station is allocated a dedicated frequency band to transmit its data. This frequency band is strictly reserved for that specific station for the entire duration of the connection.
*   **Guard Bands:** To prevent neighboring frequencies from bleeding into each other and causing adjacent channel interference, small slices of unused frequency called **guard bands** are introduced between the allocated channels.
*   **Characteristics:** All stations transmit continuously in time over their designated frequency band. Because transmission is continuous, there is no need for time-alignment or synchronization between users. FDMA traditionally works with analog continuous signals, has a relatively low data rate, and is somewhat limited in flexibility.

---

### **2. TDMA (Time Division Multiple Access)**
TDMA allows multiple users to share the **same frequency band** by dividing the transmission time into discrete, non-overlapping **time slots**.
*   **The Concept:** Instead of having their own frequency channel, each user is granted full access to the entire channel's bandwidth, but only during their specifically allocated, repeating time slot.
*   **Guard Time:** To prevent overlap and interference due to propagation delays, small buffer periods called **guard times** are inserted between the adjacent time slots.
*   **Synchronization:** Because multiple users must take turns on the exact same frequency, strict **time synchronization** is required so that each mobile station knows precisely when its slot begins and ends.
*   **Characteristics:** Data is transmitted in short "bursts" rather than a continuous stream. This burst transmission extends battery life since the mobile device's transmitter is powered down and only active during its assigned fraction of time. TDMA is moderately flexible and supports medium data rates.

---

### **3. CDMA (Code Division Multiple Access)**
CDMA takes a completely different approach: it allows all users to transmit **simultaneously** over the **entire frequency spectrum all the time**.
*   **The Concept:** Instead of separating users by frequency (FDMA) or time (TDMA), CDMA separates users by assigning each mobile station a mathematically unique, orthogonal **code sequence** (or codeword). 
*   **The Mechanism:** The transmitter mixes the user's data with this high-speed code sequence before broadcasting it. The receiver, which knows the unique code of the desired sender, uses it to extract and reconstruct the original signal while treating all other simultaneous transmissions on the frequency as negligible background noise.
*   **Guards:** Because of its complex combined sharing of time and frequency, CDMA systems require both **guard bands and guard times** for comprehensive channel isolation.
*   **Characteristics:** CDMA is highly flexible, provides excellent spectral capacity (supporting a large number of users within a wide bandwidth), does not require time synchronization between users, and offers highly secure transmission since channels are extremely difficult to decode without knowing the exact code sequence.

---

### **Comparison of FDMA, TDMA, and CDMA**

Based on your Unit 3 lecture notes, here is a detailed, direct comparison of these three multiple access techniques:

| Specification | FDMA | TDMA | CDMA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Full Form** | Frequency Division Multiple Access | Time Division Multiple Access | Code Division Multiple Access |
| **Sharing Resource** | Shares **Bandwidth** (spectrum split into frequency bands) | Shares **Time** (spectrum shared in dedicated time slots) | Shares **Both** Bandwidth and Time simultaneously |
| **Use of Codewords** | Not required | Not required | **Necessary** (orthogonal codes distinguish users) |
| **Guard Requirements** | Requires **Guard Bands** | Requires **Guard Time** | Requires **Both** Guard Bands and Guard Time |
| **Synchronization** | **Not required** | **Strictly required** between stations | **Not required** between general users *(though system-wide timing is used)* |
| **Typical Data Rate** | **Low** data rate per user | **Medium** data rate (64 kbps to 120 Mbps) | **High** data rate capability |
| **Transfer Mode** | Continuous signal | Bursts of signals | Digital signal |
| **Flexibility** | Little flexibility | Moderate flexibility | **Highly flexible** |
| **Hardware / Setup** | Simple hardware resources; easy to set up. | Complex frequency/slot allocation; requires equalizers for high data rates. | High hardware complexity; equipment tends to be more expensive. |
