# 5. Explain Cell Sectoring as a cell improvement technique. Discuss its advantages and disadvantages.

In our previous discussion about network capacity, we touched on **cell splitting**, which physically divides a congested macrocell into smaller microcells. 

**Cell sectoring** is the second major technique used by network operators to increase channel capacity and meet growing subscriber demands. While both techniques aim to solve congestion, they approach the problem differently. 

### **What is Cell Sectoring?**
Instead of physically sub-dividing a cell into smaller hexagonal sub-cells (which requires building completely new base stations as in cell splitting), **cell sectoring** divides a single cell logically into wedge-shaped sectors. 
*   **Wedge Partitioning:** Cells are typically split at an angle of **\\(120^{\circ}\\)** (yielding 3 sectors) or **\\(60^{\circ}\\)** (yielding 6 sectors).
*   **Directional Antennas:** Rather than using a single omnidirectional antenna that transmits signals equally in all directions, sectoring uses **directional antennas** at the base station to target radio coverage directly into each specific wedge.
*   **Physical Area:** Unlike cell splitting, **the cell radius remains completely unchanged** after sectoring, even though the co-channel reuse ratio decreases. In your lecture notes, these sectored cells are also referred to as microcells.

---

### **Advantages of Cell Sectoring**
*   **Improved Signal Quality:** Sectoring increases the overall **signal-to-interference ratio (S/I)**, which successfully reduces co-channel and adjacent interference without degrading general system performance.
*   **Reduced Cluster Size:** Because the S/I ratio is improved, the network can operate with a **smaller cluster size (\\(N\\))**. Reducing the cluster size increases frequency reuse across the entire system by reducing the total number of cells required in each cluster.
*   **Spectral Capacity Expansion:** It allows operators to multiply total channel capacity precisely where it is needed without changing the physical cell boundaries.
*   **Easier Allocation:** It makes the process of **assigning frequency channels** to different cells and sectors relatively easier.

---

### **Disadvantages of Cell Sectoring**
*   **Hardware Complexity:** Implementing sectoring requires mounting multiple **directional antennas per base station**, which increases infrastructure complexity and equipment overhead.
*   **Reduced Trunking Efficiency:** Dividing the cell's total channel pool into smaller, isolated channel groups (one for each wedge) decreases trunking efficiency. Because channels are locked to specific sectors, a sector can experience congestion even if an adjacent sector in the same cell has idle channels.
*   **Risk of Traffic Loss:** If excessive interference occurs, it can lead to traffic loss within the affected sectors.
*   **Frequent Handoffs:** Because sectoring reduces the physical "working area" of each active channel group, mobile devices cross sector boundaries much more often. This increases the overall frequency of **handoffs** (specifically *intra-cell handoffs* from one sector to another) that the MSC must process, placing a heavier signaling burden on the network.
