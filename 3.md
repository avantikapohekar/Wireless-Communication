# Describe cell, frequency reuse, cell size, and cell splitting.

### **The Cell**
A **cell** is a discrete geographic coverage zone that forms the basic building block of a wireless cellular network. 
*   **Infrastructure:** Each cell is served by its own localized base station (such as a cell tower or transceiver station) that communicates directly with mobile devices via radio signals.
*   **Shape:** Geometrically, cells are designed as **regular hexagons**. Although radio signals naturally propagate outward in a circular pattern, circular cells create overlapping coverage or leave gaps when tiled. Hexagons are the ideal mathematical choice for network planning because they can tile a geographic area seamlessly without any overlaps or gaps, while closely approximating a circle.

### **Frequency Reuse**
**Frequency reuse** is the cellular design technique where the **exact same radio frequencies** are used simultaneously in different cells to maximize network capacity and support millions of users.
*   **Clusters:** The total available frequency spectrum is divided among a set of cells grouped into a **cluster**. This cluster pattern is continuously repeated across the network.
*   **Co-Channel Interference:** To prevent interference, cells using the same frequencies (known as co-channel cells) must be **geographically separated** from one another. The distance between these co-channel cells, relative to the cell's radius and the cluster size, determines the amount of co-channel interference.

### **Cell Size (Cellular Hierarchy)**
To efficiently manage coverage and subscriber capacity across varying population densities, networks deploy a layered **cellular hierarchy** of different cell sizes:
*   **Mega cells:** The largest cell type, covering nationwide ranges of hundreds of kilometers, commonly used in satellite systems.
*   **Macrocells:** Large cells covering several kilometers (such as rural areas, wide highways, or metropolitan areas).
*   **Microcells:** Smaller cells covering a few hundred meters, typically used in congested urban streets.
*   **Picocells:** Small networks with ranges of a few tens of meters, often used inside buildings or for local area networks (WLANs).
*   **Femtocells:** The smallest cell unit, covering only a few meters to provide localized indoor coverage in homes or small businesses.

### **Cell Splitting**
**Cell splitting** is a method used to expand network capacity when a specific coverage area experiences a surge in subscribers and hits its capacity limits.
*   **How it works:** A highly congested, large cell (such as a macrocell) is divided into multiple **smaller, localized cells** (such as microcells or picocells). This involves introducing a new, smaller cell midway between existing co-channel cells.
*   **Outcome:** By adding more localized base stations to the same physical area, operators increase the number of channels available. This successfully boosts overall network capacity where user density is highest and helps extend coverage to hard-to-reach pockets.
